GAME CODEBASE AUDIT — 4 October 2026
====================================
Audit first; only confirmed CRITICAL defects are authorized for fixes in this pass.
The findings below were recorded before changing production code.

Scope and evidence
------------------
Reviewed the simulation orchestrator; every character-specific simulation system;
shared movement, damage, contact, grapple, stun, projectile, status and structure
systems; fighter definitions and validation; life, tournament and elimination
transitions; rollback/history/hash handling; room/championship transport and
timeouts; client loading/navigation/input and frame loop; rendering pools,
interpolation, asset ownership, audio lifecycle and existing regression coverage.
This is a code/test audit, not a claim that every visual asset was inspected.

Baseline: 690 tests, 684 passed, 6 failed. Added an all-roster replay/invariant test:
256 ordered fighter pairings, 240 forward frames plus a 120-frame replay each.
All 256 passed finite-state and exact same-runtime snapshot replay checks.
This does not prove cross-browser floating-point equivalence, exhaust every
ability interaction, or replace long multiplayer soak tests on real devices.

Findings: 1 CRITICAL, 1 HIGH, 6 MEDIUM, 2 LOW.
Locations below are repository-relative paths and named functions, which remain
usable after edits. Reproductions using state setup isolate reachable conditions;
they are not claims that every issue was manually reproduced through the UI.

CRITICAL
========
C1 — Bozo clone-growth renderer crashes in player slots 7 and 8
Problem: a valid 7/8-player match can stop rendering and advancing entirely.
Where: apps/client/src/rendering/HairCloneGrowth.ts, bundles field and update(),
       indexing this.bundles[owner*3+i]; apps/client/src/main.ts, loop().
Reproduce: start a 7- or 8-player FFA, put Bozo in slot 7 or 8, obtain full energy,
           activate his clone super. At growth frame 4 onward the renderer throws.
           Headless reproduction with slot 7 at move='clones', moveFrame=5 gives
           TypeError: Cannot set properties of undefined (setting 'visible').
Cause: only 18 strand bundles exist (6 players × 3), while gameplay permits 8.
       The exception escapes rendering before requestAnimationFrame(loop) runs.
       Online peers can subsequently stall while waiting for the affected client.
Safest fix: provision the three bundles for every supported seat, preserving
            the existing animation math, indices, timing and companion gameplay.
Regression risk: LOW. Six additional visual meshes; no simulation/network changes.
                 Test all 2–8 roster sizes and all seats, including simultaneous
                 casters and disappearance when interrupted.
Status at audit completion: confirmed, queued for the critical-only fix below.

HIGH
====
H1 — Nikalz shock/meteor waves bypass recovery and escape protection
Problem: waves damage knocked-down/getting-up targets and force a full new stun
         even when the shared anti-combo system grants escape immunity.
Where: packages/game-core/simulation/NikalzSystem.ts, advanceNikalz() wave loop;
       packages/game-core/combat/PeriodicHit.ts and HitstunSystem.ts.
Reproduce: put an enemy inside a forehead wave with state GetUp or Knockdown.
           It loses 125 health and becomes AirHitStun with stun=120. Give an Idle
           enemy hitstunImmuneUntil=100 at frame 0: the same wave still sets 120
           stun. These outcomes were reproduced directly. Repeated wave contacts
           can therefore defeat the intended escape protection.
Cause: the wave eligibility check uses isActive/isInvulnerable, omitting the
       shared direct-hit recovery policy. After periodicHit() respects immunity,
       the caller unconditionally writes nikalzStunUntil/stun/move/velocity/state.
Safest fix: use the common eligibility rule and apply Nikalz's private stun clock
            only when the common reaction decision allows stun. Keep current
            damage, wave range and approved full-stun duration for eligible hits.
            Test fifth-hit escape, existing immunity, armor, Gym Bro, recovery,
            airborne targets and multiple waves before shipping.
Regression risk: MEDIUM. Several reaction policies meet here; do not simply
                 remove the private deadline or change stun durations globally.
Status: NOT changed; outside the critical-only repair scope.

MEDIUM
======
M1 — Bee entities have no lifetime or population limit
Problem: missed swarms accumulate for as long as their owner stays alive. This
         grows collision work and every retained rollback snapshot.
Where: packages/game-core/simulation/NikalzSystem.ts, spawnBees()/advanceNikalz().
Reproduce: cast Bees repeatedly away from the opponent in infinite-energy
           training. Swarms disappear only on contact or owner inactivity.
           A probe kept all 200 swarms alive at frame 100000.
Cause: unlike pooled projectiles, BeeSwarm has no expiry or capacity check.
       Limiting each house to 10 productions does not bound direct special casts.
Safest fix: establish an explicit approved lifetime/cap policy, then implement it
            in snapshot state with deterministic eviction or cast rejection.
Regression risk: MEDIUM; despawning/capping swarms changes move persistence, so
                 obtain the intended policy rather than invent a balance value.

M2 — Simultaneous lethal Dodo music pulses favor earlier seats
Problem: a later caster's due pulse is suppressed when an earlier pulse kills it
         in the same frame; other simultaneous attacks use collected contacts.
Where: packages/game-core/simulation/DodoSystem.ts, advanceDodo().
Reproduce: two Dodos 20 units apart, health=20 each, weaponUntil=1000, frame=1,
           pulses due. advanceDodo() leaves health [20, 0], not a mutual hit.
Cause: the loop applies damage immediately, then checks isActive for the next
       source after its health was changed by the earlier source.
Safest fix: decide/document simultaneous-pulse policy; if mutual contacts are
            intended, collect eligible pulses before committing damage, as the
            shared continuous-attack system already does.
Regression risk: MEDIUM. Changes only same-frame trades but can change winners.

M3 — Match-end cleanup omits Nikalz entities
Problem: bees and forehead waves remain in result-state snapshots. They no
         longer deal damage, but stale entities remain retained after the match.
Where: packages/game-core/simulation/LifeSystem.ts, resolveKnockouts();
       GameSimulation.ts, advanceFrame() early result return; also review
       TournamentSystem.ts/EliminationSystem.ts finalization paths together.
Reproduce: have active bees, end the opponent's last life, then step result.
           Probe still contained 200 bees after the result step.
Cause: life cleanup clears ordinary projectiles/hazards/clones/structures but
       not these arrays. advanceNikalz's non-fight cleanup is unreachable after
       advanceFrame's result return. resetPositions does clear them next round.
Safest fix: explicitly clear these transient arrays at terminal match finalization
            and add end-state tests across normal/tournament/elimination modes.
Regression risk: LOW–MEDIUM. Preserve deliberate result-screen visual effects.

M4 — Clone-growth visual ownership is missed during renderer teardown
Problem: cloned textures/resources belonging to preview fighter visuals do not
         receive their owner-specific dispose calls on renderer teardown.
Where: apps/client/src/rendering/SceneRenderer.ts, dispose();
       apps/client/src/rendering/HairCloneGrowth.ts, dispose().
Reproduce: create Bozo clone previews, then tear down/recreate the scene (e.g.
           development hot reload). HairCloneGrowth.dispose is never invoked.
Cause: SceneRenderer traverses mesh geometry/materials, but omits cloneGrowth's
       explicit cleanup; material disposal does not dispose owned textures.
Safest fix: invoke the owned component's disposal and test texture disposal
            counters; avoid disposing globally shared source textures twice.
Regression risk: LOW, with ownership tests. This is not a per-frame leak claim.

M5 — Regression suite has six unresolved failures
Problem: the default suite is red, making a genuine new regression harder to
         distinguish from accepted changes and incomplete test mocks.
Where/reproduce: run tests/*.test.ts. Failures:
  - architecture-baseline.test.ts: stored movement-facing hashes differ.
  - architecture-contracts.test.ts: HairSystem hard-codes characterId==='bozo'.
  - audit-20260930-fixes.test.ts: football/house test expects a 65-health house to
    collapse after one hit, but football currently deals the approved 52.
  - audit-loading.test.ts: texture mock returns an image-less THREE.Texture for
    new air art; framesFrom reads null.width. This is not a proven production
    loading crash because actual successful texture loading supplies an image.
  - jopa.test.ts: expects old White tee, current requested skin is Black polo.
  - special-energy.test.ts: expects +110 health, current meditation definition
    heals 85 per 120 frames, hence +170 after 240 frames.
Cause: balance/art/architecture changes were not reconciled with fixtures/mocks.
Safest fix: update clearly stale expectations against approved values, complete
            the loading mock, and review each changed behavioral trace before
            accepting a new baseline. Never blindly regenerate all golden hashes.
Regression risk: MEDIUM; careless baseline updates can bless unintended changes.
Status: left visible; no balance values changed to make tests green.

M6 — Shared systems retain character identities and duplicate lifecycle policy
Problem: adding a fighter/ability requires coordinated edits in multiple central
         places; missed cleanup and divergent stun rules are already observable.
Where: GameSimulation.updateFighter/activation chain; LifeSystem manual cleanup;
       HairSystem.summonClones (Bozo-specific health offset); JopaSystem hard-coded
       registry references; JompSystem and NikalzSystem private reaction clocks.
Reproduce: architectural guard test fails on HairSystem's Bozo identity branch.
           Add a new optional transient field: respawn reconstructs it safely,
           but terminal cleanup still needs a separate manual decision.
Cause: behavior flags, character IDs, move IDs, state strings and several timer
       implementations coexist without a single lifecycle/interrupt contract.
Safest fix: first add lifecycle contract tests per mechanic; migrate one narrowly
            scoped behavior at a time only when a real fix/addition needs it.
Regression risk: HIGH for a wholesale refactor, LOW–MEDIUM for tested extraction.
Status: working-but-fragile architecture, not justification to rewrite combat.

LOW
===
L1 — Generic hazard audio damage attribution uses the victim's burn owner
Problem: a generic damaging hazard can report source=-1 or an unrelated attacker
         and move='@burn', despite its real owner/move being known.
Where: packages/game-core/simulation/StatusSystem.ts, damage() and hazard loop.
Reproduce: an unburned opponent takes a generic hazard tick; inspect DamageApplied
           metadata. The helper takes f.burnOwner rather than h.owner/h.move.
Cause: burn and generic hazard damage reuse a helper with burn-specific metadata.
Safest fix: pass damage source metadata explicitly from each caller; keep damage
            arithmetic and eligibility unchanged. Add an event-attribution test.
Regression risk: LOW; presentation metadata only.

L2 — Finished-room timeout exemption trusts unverified client claims
Problem: all clients can claim the same finished frame and keep a started room
         exempt from inactivity expiry while sockets continue answering pings.
Where: apps/server/src/rooms.ts, finished()/expireInactive().
Reproduce: ready a room; send input frame 2 and finished frame 2 from each peer,
           then send no more inputs. Existing audit-timeouts.test.ts confirms
           membership survives 300 seconds even though frame 2 is still intro.
Cause: ordinary rooms relay simulation rather than authoritatively verifying a
       result; the exemption has no separate result-room retention deadline.
Safest fix: define a generous result-room retention/close policy independent of
            gameplay input timeout. Do not forfeit legitimate result spectators.
Regression risk: LOW–MEDIUM. Primarily resource/abuse hardening; does not let one
                 honest-match opponent alone disable everyone's timeout.

Additional working-but-fragile areas
-----------------------------------
- Rendering capacities are independent constants (18/32/64/8). C1 demonstrates
  why all roster sizes and companion counts need coverage when limits change.
- State is represented by a state string plus move, stun, grapple, trap and many
  absolute deadlines. Several systems can overwrite reactions in one frame.
  Keep interaction tests around these priorities before adding another status.
- Simulation order is policy: some systems collect contacts, others mutate actors
  sequentially; hitstop pauses some clocks while absolute deadlines continue.
  Both are deterministic locally, but not automatically equivalent or fair.
- contentVersion fingerprints core/networking/protocol and definition data. The
  generated revision and Vite watcher are useful safeguards; future authoritative
  server-rule changes need a compatibility policy too. Same-runtime replay alone
  is not evidence of cross-engine determinism.
- AssetManager retains raw bytes while TextureCache retains decoded source
  textures. Both have teardown paths, but there is no session memory budget for
  visiting every skin. Measure actual browser/GPU memory before changing caching.

Systems with useful existing safeguards (no new critical defect established)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Projectile slots reset optional fields on reuse; returning weapons have expiry
  and owner-death cancellation. Room send queues have a hard buffer budget.
- History/input maps are pruned; hash checks use settled history; terminal rollback
  errors stop simulation/input scheduling rather than continuing a divergent match.
- Room input validation enforces sequential frames and bounded peer lead;
  disconnects preserve seat indices, and championship packets use match tokens.
- Frame-based ability timers live in snapshots; gameplay does not use wall-clock
  callbacks. Input/socket/audio teardown removes listeners or cancels timers.
- Respawn reconstructs fighter fields while preserving selected life contracts;
  loading uses a navigation revision to avoid late completion reopening a match.
- Rejoining an already-started room is rejected. There is no seamless reconnect
  feature to certify; controller reconnection and socket forfeits are separate.

FOLLOW-UP — all-severity repairs authorized and completed
=========================================================
The user's subsequent “fix it all” request supersedes the critical-only stop.
All concrete findings above now have targeted repairs. The general architecture
risk notes remain maintenance guidance, not a claim that every future interaction
has been proven safe.

H1: PeriodicHit now returns whether it applied a reaction. Nikalz waves use shared
    direct-hit eligibility and write their private stun/launch state only when
    that reaction is allowed. Damage and eligible 120/360-frame stun durations
    remain unchanged. Tests cover recovery, escape immunity, fifth-hit escape,
    stance armor, Gym Bro victims and protected landing stun.
M1: Missed swarms retain their existing lifetime. A deterministic limit of 100
    swarms per owner replaces the oldest owned swarm on overflow; other owners
    are untouched. This is a resource-safety policy chosen for this repair, not
    a previously approved balance value. No new expiry timer was introduced.
    Hive production remains 10 swarms per house at its existing interval.
    Tests cover owner isolation, persistence and exact replay at the limit.
M2: Dodo music contacts are collected before applying damage, allowing same-frame
    lethal trades without seat-order suppression. Damage/radius/interval unchanged.
M3: New CombatLifecycle.clearCombatEntities is shared by normal knockout endings,
    tournament forfeits and elimination endings/breaks. It clears bees/waves as
    well as the existing entity groups. Owner death also removes owned bees/waves
    immediately. Tests cover ordinary endings, competition forfeits and life loss.
M4: SceneRenderer calls cloneGrowth.dispose and detaches its root before generic
    traversal. A real texture disposal-counter test verifies all 24 preview
    textures are disposed exactly once in an eight-player case.
M5: All six formerly failing tests have been reconciled:
    - Football-house fixture uses current football damage (52).
    - Jopa skin expectation uses Black polo.
    - Meditation expects the existing 85-per-120-frame definition (+170/240).
    - Loading mock supplies image data to fall/air raster decoders, while keeping
      unrelated atlas mocks out of raster validation. Retry behavior still tested.
    - Shared companion health no longer branches on Bozo's identity.
    - Only the 19 stale movement-facing baseline entries were updated. All 100
      current traces were compared to the original audit's captured actual traces:
      ZERO trace changes resulted from this repair. Existing focused tests for
      dives, grapples, mode expiration, burns, returning weapons, beams and turrets
      remained green. No runtime values were changed to fit a golden fixture.
M6: Narrow architectural repair, preserving intended behavior: terminal entity
    cleanup is shared; reaction eligibility is returned by the shared hit system;
    companion health ratio/offset live in validated character definitions.
    Renamed-character tests prove health no longer depends on a hard-coded ID.
    Bozo retains floor(maxHealth/3)-60; Dodo retains floor(maxHealth*.4)-120.
    Character-specific controllers still exist; wholesale replacement was avoided.
L1: Generic hazard damage events now carry the hazard's owner and move. Burn ticks
    retain burn metadata. A regression test verifies pentagram attribution/damage.
L2: Finished rooms retain the existing short-term timeout exemption, but expire
